# Validity threats

Sort the threat before writing the finding: naming the category tells the researcher which part
of the design to change. Report a threat only when the record shows it and `limitations` does
not already declare it.

## Internal validity — does the study support its own causal story?

- **Selection** — the groups differed before anything was done to them.
- **History** — something outside the study happened between measurements.
- **Maturation** — the units changed on their own over the study's span.
- **Instrumentation** — the measure itself changed between waves or between coders.
- **Regression to the mean** — units were selected because they were extreme.
- **Attrition** — who dropped out is related to the outcome.
- **Testing** — being measured changed what was measured.

## External validity — does it reach past the sample?

- **Population** — the sampled group is not the group the claims speak about.
- **Setting** — one site, one platform, one season, generalized to all.
- **Time** — the finding is reported as standing, on data from one period.
- **Treatment variation** — the intervention as delivered is not the intervention as named.

## Construct validity — is the thing measured the thing named?

- The construct is named in the claims but operationalized nowhere.
- A single item stands in for a multi-dimensional construct.
- The measure is reactive: participants can tell what is wanted.
- Two constructs are measured by the same instrument and treated as independent.

## Statistical conclusion validity — do the numbers support the wording?

- The sample size was never justified, and the null result is read as absence of an effect.
- Assumptions of the test are neither checked nor mentioned.
- Effects are reported as significant with no effect size or interval.
- Many comparisons were run and only the surviving ones are reported.
- The analysis unit is not the sampling unit (clustered data analysed as independent).
