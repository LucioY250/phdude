# Adversarial checks

Run down the list once per target. Each line is a question with an id-shaped answer; a "yes I
worried about that" with nothing to point at is not a finding.

## Alternatives to name

- **Confounding** — a third variable produces both sides of the association.
- **Reverse causation** — the outcome plausibly drives the predictor.
- **Selection** — who ended up in the data explains the pattern.
- **Measurement artefact** — the instrument, not the world, produced the difference.
- **Survivorship** — the units that failed are not in the sample.
- **Regression to the mean** — the extreme group moved back on its own.
- **Simpson's paradox** — the aggregate reverses within subgroups.
- **Publication or reporting bias** — for a review, what would not have been found.

## Comparison integrity

- Is the baseline recorded as an object, or only described in prose?
- Is it the strongest available baseline, or the most convenient one?
- Were both sides measured with the same instrument, on the same data, under the same conditions?
- Are the tuning and selection effort comparable across the sides?
- Is the difference reported with an interval or a spread, not a single number?

## Argument integrity

- Does any conclusion appear that no claim in the record supports?
- Does any recorded claim that would weaken the conclusion go unmentioned?
- Does a limitation appear in the limitations section and nowhere in the wording of the claims?
- Does the abstract state something stronger than the results section does?
- Does a number in the prose trace to a `FACT-` or `RESULT-` that still holds?

## What not to report

- Style, voice and phrasing: that is `phdude prose`, and duplicating it here buries the findings
  that matter.
- Anything you would have to leave the workspace to check.
- A finding the context already lists as recorded. Disagreeing with a recorded finding is fine —
  say so and say why, as its own finding.
