# Clinical reporting standards

Short summaries of what each checklist asks for, so a review can name the missing item rather
than the checklist. These are summaries written for this skill, not the checklists themselves —
consult the published statement before submitting to a journal that requires one.

## CONSORT — randomized controlled trials

What a reviewer looks for first:

1. The title and abstract identify the study as randomized.
2. Eligibility criteria, and the settings and dates of recruitment.
3. The interventions for each group, in enough detail to replicate.
4. Pre-specified primary and secondary outcomes, and any changes made after the trial started.
5. How the sample size was determined.
6. How the allocation sequence was generated, concealed, and implemented.
7. Who was blinded, and how — or an explicit statement that nobody was.
8. A participant flow diagram: randomized, received the intervention, analysed, lost.
9. Results per group with an effect size and its precision, not a p-value alone.
10. All harms and unintended effects, including the ones that were absent.

The three omissions worth a `block`: no flow diagram, no allocation concealment described, and
outcomes reported that were not the ones pre-specified.

## STROBE — observational studies (cohort, case-control, cross-sectional)

1. The design is named in the title or abstract in the words the field uses.
2. Eligibility, sources, and methods of selection for every group, with the follow-up for cohorts
   and the matching criteria for case-control.
3. Every variable defined: outcomes, exposures, predictors, confounders, effect modifiers.
4. The data source and measurement method for each variable, with comparability across groups.
5. How bias was addressed, how the study size was arrived at, and how quantitative variables were
   grouped.
6. All statistical methods, including how confounding was controlled and how missing data were
   handled.
7. Participant numbers at each stage, with reasons for non-participation.
8. Unadjusted and confounder-adjusted estimates side by side, with their precision.

The recurring omission: confounders named in the discussion but never measured or adjusted for.

## PRISMA — systematic reviews and meta-analyses

1. The review question in a structured form, and a registration or protocol if there is one.
2. Eligibility criteria, and every information source with the date last searched.
3. The full search strategy for at least one database, reproducibly.
4. The selection and data-collection process, including how many reviewers and how disagreements
   were settled.
5. A flow diagram: records identified, screened, excluded with reasons, included.
6. A risk-of-bias assessment per study, and how it fed into the synthesis.
7. Synthesis methods, heterogeneity, and any sensitivity analyses.
8. Certainty of the evidence for each outcome, and the limitations of the review itself.

The recurring omission: a search strategy that cannot be re-run, which makes the review
unrepeatable regardless of how the synthesis was done.
