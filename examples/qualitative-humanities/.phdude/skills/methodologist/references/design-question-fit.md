# Design–question fit

What each design can and cannot answer. Use it to decide whether a `METH-` reaches the `RQ-` it
is attached to; when it does not, the finding is `block`, because every claim behind that
question inherits the gap.

| Design | Answers | Does not answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-sectional survey | What is the case now, across a group; how two measures co-vary | Whether one thing caused another; how anything changed |
| Longitudinal / panel | How a measure moves over time within the same units | Why it moved, unless the design isolates the cause |
| Cohort study | Whether an exposed group differs in outcome from an unexposed one | Causation without adjustment for the confounders that were measured, and nothing about the ones that were not |
| Case-control | Whether cases and controls differ in prior exposure | Incidence, prevalence, or absolute risk |
| Randomized experiment | Whether the manipulated variable changed the outcome, in this population | Whether the effect holds outside the sampled population or setting |
| Quasi-experiment | The same, weakened by whatever assignment was not random | Anything the assignment mechanism can also explain |
| Case study | How something works in one setting, in depth | Prevalence, generalization, or comparison to a population |
| Systematic review | What the recorded literature collectively reports | Anything the search strategy could not have reached |
| Simulation / benchmark | How a system behaves under the modelled conditions | Behaviour under conditions the model does not represent |

Three questions per pair:

1. Does the design's unit of analysis match the unit the question asks about?
2. Does the time structure match? A question with a "leads to", "increases" or "after" in it
   needs a design that observes both moments.
3. Does the comparison the question implies exist in the design at all? A claim that X is better
   than Y needs a Y that was measured, not assumed.
