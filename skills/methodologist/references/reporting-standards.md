# Reporting standards

A checklist is a review instrument, not a formatting rule: each item exists because reviewers
found the omission changed how a study reads. Check the manuscript against the checklist the
applied pack recommends (`recommended_checks`) and report the *items that are missing*, each as
its own finding with the section it belongs in.

| Standard | Applies to | Where its summary lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CONSORT | Randomized controlled trials | medicine pack, `references/reporting-standards.md` |
| STROBE | Observational studies: cohort, case-control, cross-sectional | medicine pack, `references/reporting-standards.md` |
| PRISMA | Systematic reviews and meta-analyses | medicine pack, `references/reporting-standards.md` |
| Power analysis | Any quantitative design making a comparison | quantitative pack |
| Assumption checks | Any inferential test | quantitative pack |
| Effect-size reporting | Any quantitative comparison | quantitative pack |
| Saturation justification | Qualitative sampling | qualitative pack |
| Inter-coder reliability | Coded qualitative data | qualitative pack |
| Reflexivity statement | Interpretive qualitative work | qualitative pack |
| Risk-of-bias assessment | Systematic reviews | systematic-review pack |
| Baseline comparison, ablation | Computational evaluation | computer-science pack |

What is common to all of them, and what to check first when no pack applies:

1. The question and the design are stated before the results.
2. Who or what was studied, how many, and how they were selected.
3. What was measured, with what, and by whom.
4. What was done to the data before it was analysed, including exclusions and their reasons.
5. Every planned analysis, including the ones that returned nothing.
6. The limitations the authors themselves see.
