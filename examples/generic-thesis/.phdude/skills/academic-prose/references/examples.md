# Before and after

Concrete pairs. Each "after" is what the same content looks like when it rests on the workspace
rather than on register. The markers and keys are illustrative: use the ones your context lists.

## The PRD §29.1 example

Before:

> Recent studies have increasingly demonstrated the significant importance of note-taking
> applications in modern education.

After:

> In a survey of 312 undergraduates, 214 took lecture notes on a phone rather than a laptop
> [@lopez2023].

The first sentence names no study, no number and no population, and "demonstrated" claims more
than a literature gesture can support. The second can be checked, and disagreed with.

## Vague literature

Before:

> A growing body of research suggests that adoption varies by institution.

After:

> Adoption ranged from 41% to 78% across the three surveys [@alpha2025; @beta2025; @gamma2025].
> <!-- claim: CLAIM-… -->

## A verb outrunning its evidence

Before, on a claim in state `candidate`:

> The data demonstrates that recruitment channel drives adoption.

After:

> The data suggests that recruitment channel is associated with adoption
> [@beta2025]. <!-- claim: CLAIM-… -->

Two changes, not one: "demonstrates" became "suggests" because the claim is a candidate, and
"drives" became "is associated with" because the design is correlational.

## An unmarked number

Before:

> Roughly 300 students took part.

After:

> 312 students took part <!-- fact: FACT-… -->.

"Roughly" was doing the work of not knowing. The workspace knows.

## Stacked hedges

Before:

> The intervention may possibly indicate that engagement could improve somewhat.

After:

> The intervention is associated with a 12-point rise in engagement
> [@lopez2023]. <!-- result: RESULT-… -->

Three hedges is not caution, it is an absent claim. One hedge, at the strength the evidence
supports, is a finding.

## Transition density

Before:

> Furthermore, adoption rose. Moreover, retention held. Additionally, the cohorts matched.

After:

> Adoption rose and retention held. The two cohorts matched on every recorded covariate.

None of those connectives marked a turn. The sentences carry the same content and the reader can
now see where the real turn would be.

## A symmetrical list

Before:

> - Adoption is slow among small firms.
> - Adoption is uneven across channels.
> - Adoption is costly to measure.

After:

> Adoption is slow among small firms and uneven across recruitment channels [@beta2025].
> Measuring it is itself expensive, which is why the three surveys disagree on the
> denominator. <!-- claim: CLAIM-… -->

The list enumerated; the paragraph argues.

## An intensifier with nothing behind it

Before:

> This novel approach yields robust improvements.

After:

> No prior survey recorded phone-first note-taking, and this one does
> [@lopez2023]. Engagement rose by 12 points <!-- result: RESULT-… -->.

"Novel" became the recorded search that found nothing prior; "robust" became the number.

## A revision that would be blocked

Original:

> Adoption is not uniform across the 312 firms [@smith2020]. <!-- claim: CLAIM-… -->

A revision that reads better and blocks:

> Adoption varies across firms.

It drops the negation, the number, the citation and the claim marker. Four separate blocks from
`gate-meaning`, and every one of them is a change of meaning wearing the clothes of a style fix.

A revision that passes:

> Across the 312 firms surveyed, adoption is not uniform [@smith2020]. <!-- claim: CLAIM-… -->
